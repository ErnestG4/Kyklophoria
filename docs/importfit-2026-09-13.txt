What it says
------------
`in band` is the ceiling: energy at harmonics 1..64 over the file's total. No
representation at this K can beat it and none needs to. The median waveform is
inside 64 harmonics to four figures.

`kept now` is what import actually keeps — the sine half after the rotation
search has done its best. The gap between the two is the cosine half, thrown
away, and it is the larger of the two losses for most of the corpus: 7.3 points
of the mean against 2.4 for the band limit.

The correlations are the numbers to argue from. Energy flatters a projection,
because the part discarded is the phase relationship between partials rather
than a scaling — and that relationship is what makes a saw a saw rather than a
stack of sines with a saw's spectrum (README: 0.79 correlation, measured).
Storing phase would move the median waveform correlation from 0.976 to 1.000,
the tenth percentile from 0.865 to 0.980, and the fraction of the corpus that
comes out under 0.90 from one in six to one in thirty-three.

How the correlation is computed
-------------------------------
sqrt(kept/total), by Parseval — the inner product of the file with its own
sine-only projection picks up exactly the kept terms. Checked against the
direct time-domain correlation on sample waveforms and exact to four figures,
because a derivation is not a measurement.

A correction
------------
The first version of this file compared cycleToNode's `fit` against a band
fraction and concluded from their percentiles that the band limit was not the
binding constraint. The direction was right and the comparison was not: `fit`
is a fraction of *in-band* energy (`tot` in cycleToNode sums h = 1..k only)
while the band fraction is of total energy, so the two columns had different
denominators, and percentiles of two distributions do not compose. Rerun with
one denominator, and with the correlation added, which is what the question was
actually about.

Caveats
-------
AKWF is not a neutral sample of "waveforms people import". It is curated, heavy
on single cycles ripped from hardware, and it is what our own README points
people at. It is the right corpus for this question and it is still one corpus.

Correlation is not perception either. It weights every partial by energy, and
ears do not. It is a better proxy than energy and it is still a proxy — which
is why the page can now play a waveform three ways (as imported, band-limited,
as rendered) and let somebody listen.
