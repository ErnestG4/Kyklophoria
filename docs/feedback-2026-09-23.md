## The three-way prototype (ModalBake bench/threeway: a recorded / b synth / c synth + waveguide)

> The waveguide isn't being properly applied. It sounds like a separate wave
> on top of the signal instead of modulating it. Listen to Wurli C2. The
> actual recording's timbre has the signature wurli Beooooo phase fade from
> the tine and pickup. The waveguide tries to add that where the others are
> flat, but fails. I will say that overall the synth and recorded strikes are
> very similar with only the "passage" parts being problematic due to the
> "snare chain effect" of noise on top. Again the waveguide in the piano-vcsl
> sounds more like a spring playing along with it than a deeper and subtler
> modulation.

What that says, for whoever picks it up (the lead's reading, not Combust's):
- **The synthesised strike (b) is at par with the recorded attack by ear.**
  That is the green light for owning the exciter (SECONDARY above).
- **The waveguide as built is the wrong coupling.** It is summed beside the
  modes, so it is heard as a second instrument: a separate wave on the
  Wurlitzer, a spring on the piano. Spring reverb IS a dispersive delay line,
  and (c) runs up to 16 dispersion allpasses. What the ear wants is the
  timbre of the note itself evolving: on the Wurlitzer that "Beooooo" is the
  reed's decaying swing through the pickup's nonlinearity (the harmonics
  fall with the amplitude), which is the pickup stage's job (the gap form
  ModalBake already fits), not a delay line's.
- **"Snare chain" in the passages is the noise band stacking** across
  overlapping notes: twelve bands with their own decays, one set a strike,
  summing into a wash at four notes a second. It wants the noise tied to the
  contact (it lives while the hammer or finger touches, milliseconds) and
  choked with its voice on a steal.
