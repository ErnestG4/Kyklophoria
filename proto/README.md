# proto/: a synthesised exciter, and a waveguide above the modes

This is a desktop prototype, written overnight 22–23 September 2026. It is
not wired into Kyklophoria's `core/` or firmware, and it changes no world and
not `tools/export.py`. It uses the runtime as it is, through
`../runtime/kyk_resonate.h` (the verbatim copy that `make check-runtime`
keeps). The point is a three-way comparison you can listen to:

- **(a) today's runtime:** the recorded burst, with the modes coming in under its fade;
- **(b) the synthesised exciter:** the same modes, struck from t = 0 by a contact model, plus a parametric noise band;
- **(c) (b) plus a waveguide:** one loop a voice, above the bank's top mode.

Everything here is measured against each note's own recording, at the
world's scale (nothing is re-levelled). My harness's (a) is
`kykdesk`, sample for sample: 3e-8 relative, on the 4-voice passages as
well as the single notes. Numbers and WAVs are regenerated by:

    make -C proto                                   # build/proto/libkykproto.so
    ~/fmexplorer/bin/python proto/threeway.py --refit   # fits (CPU, 6 workers, ~10 min), renders, measures
    ~/fmexplorer/bin/python proto/velocity.py       # one note at four velocities, (a) and (b)
    ~/fmexplorer/bin/python proto/report.py [--md]  # the tables below
    make -C proto armcost                           # M7 instruction counts

Where things land:

- WAVs: `bench/threeway/` (git-ignored);
- the fitted numbers for each point: `bench/threeway/params/`;
- every measurement: `bench/threeway/results.json`.

Nothing derived from a recording is in this directory.

## The design

### (b) The exciter

**The contact, one model per class.**

- **Hammer (piano, Wurlitzer).** A unit mass on a felt spring,
  F = K·d^2.5, integrated at strike time (RK4, 8 steps a sample) until the
  felt lets go. The contact length at the reference swing comes from a
  keyboard law: 4 ms at A0 to 0.8 ms at C8 for the piano, and 1.5 ms for
  the Wurlitzer. It then follows the Hertz law, T ∝ v^−3/7, so a hard strike
  is shorter and a soft one longer.
- **Pluck (guitar).** A raised-cosine release 1 ms wide, its width ∝ v^−0.3.

What the rest of the voice hears of the contact is two things.

- **Its arrival** (the impulse so far).
- **A felt low-pass, 1/(1 + (f·T)²).** Each mode, noise band and the
  waveguide gets this at its own frequency, divided by the value for the
  reference strike. At the swing the recording was fitted at, that ratio is
  exactly 1 everywhere, so **the sustain is today's sustain**: the same
  modes, at the same levels and phases, once both ramps are over.

The smooth Hertz pulse's own spectrum could not be used for this. It has
nulls, and it is 60–100 dB down by 2–8 kHz, which no recording's attack is.

On the M7 the pulse would be one stored shape, stretched to T(v) (the pure
Hertz pulse is self-similar), not the ODE.

**The modes.** The strike bank is set at the fitted gains and phases, as
today, and ramped in from t = 0 by a raised cosine. Its length is fitted
from 3–26 ms on the early window. A Wurlitzer's reed takes 12–18 ms to
build; over the fit's own 3 ms it played the note 28 dB early at 5 ms. The
modes are not a driven bank: I tried driving them with the pulse, and a
mode above the pulse's bandwidth needs +60 dB of input gain, which rings
through the contact as a thump. The ramp is the honest version of "driven
from t = 0".

**The noise.** White noise through 12 log-spaced band-passes, 60 Hz–16 kHz,
followed by a 16 kHz low-pass. The low-pass is there because the top band's
skirt ran to Nyquist, where a 44.1 kHz recording has nothing. Each band has
a level, a fast decay (≥ 2 ms), a slow decay (≤ 0.1 s) and the slow part's
share.

The noise is fitted by one joint least squares over log band energy: 5 ms
frames every 2.5 ms, 0–150 ms, modes plus noise against the recording. The
fit includes the synthesis bands' leakage into the analysis bands. Two
rules came out of the measurements:

- **A floor bound.** In each band, and over 0–50 and 50–150 ms, the noise
  may not pass the recording's own floor between its partials (measured as
  specaudit measures static) by more than 3 dB. Fitted to band energy
  alone, the noise stood in for pitched energy the modes miss. On guitar G3
  that was a +15 dB burst in the fundamental's band, which doubled the
  passage's peaks, and the attack's static was +8 to +38 dB.
- **An arrival choice.** The noise arrives either with the contact or over
  the modes' ramp, whichever fits the early window better.

### (c) The waveguide above the modes (option 1)

**The loop.**

- **Delay.** One delay line a voice, a first-order Thiran for the fraction,
  and two groups of first-order allpasses for the stiffness (at most 16
  sections in all).
- **Loss.** A one-pole loss set from a decay law σ(f) = σ₁ₖ·(f/1 kHz)^β.
  The law comes from the recording's own band T60s above the bank; it is
  applied per round trip at the loop's group delay, so it holds when the
  note is transposed.
- **Crossover.** A 4th-order high-pass *inside* the loop at 0.8 × the
  crossover, so nothing under it can ring. A 2nd-order version at the
  crossover took the partials just above the bank to 0.7–0.9 a round trip,
  dead in 100 ms. There are also 2nd-order high-passes at the input and the
  output, at the crossover.
- **Loop-gain bound.** The loss is normalised against the whole loop gain
  (loss × high-pass < 1 everywhere), not its own DC gain. Capping the
  latter cost those partials 4% a round trip.

**The partials it plays.**

- **The crossover** is 1.05 × the bank's top live mode.
- **The series** is k·f0·√(1 + B·k²). f0 and B come from the recording's own
  partials above the bank, where it has 8 or more; otherwise from the bank's
  modes. They agree: C4 has B 3.8e-4 from the peaks against 3.9e-4 from the
  bank, and the bank's modes sit a median 1.5 cents from the series.
- **The band's top** is the recording's highest partial above the bank.
- **The resonance number.** The loop's k-th resonance is allowed to be the
  string's partial k + j, with j a whole number the fit picks. A stretched
  series is, over a band, close to an offset comb, and the offset is cheap.
  With j, a plain delay puts C4's partials within 0.2 of a spacing; without
  it, that took 16 allpass sections.
- **The dispersion** is a grid search in the phase domain, then checked with
  the library's own Newton on the loop as it plays.

**The input.** Hann-windowed noise over the contact (or the modes' ramp if
that is longer). It carries the energy of a unit impulse and has random
phases. A single-sample impulse put every partial in phase, and the passage
measured that click at up to 54 dB. The input has a level and a two-pole
tilt, fitted on the bands above the crossover over 0–2 s, with frames at the
recording's floor ignored. Its first 10 ms are capped at 3 dB over the
recording's.

**When it is on.** Only where the recording's own bands above the bank hold
a decay: a measured T60 of at least 0.25 s. The Wurlitzer's C4 and C5, the
Iowa C4, C6 and C7, and every guitar recording (1 s splits) have only an
attack click up there, then their floor. The gate turns the waveguide off
for those, and then (c) is (b) exactly.

The waveguide keeps (b)'s noise unchanged: the unpitched part doesn't depend
on what plays the pitched part.

### What is fitted a point

| part | numbers | from |
|---|---|---|
| contact | kind, T, p | class and keyboard law: **not fitted.** One take cannot see it (every part hears exactly the reference strike at the fitted swing). It sets only the velocity law |
| modes' ramp | 1 of 6 (3–26 ms) | early window |
| noise arrival | 1 of 2 | early window |
| noise | 12 × (level, fast decay, slow decay, share) = 48 | 0–150 ms, bounded by the floor between partials |
| waveguide tuning | f0, B | recording's partials above the bank (bank's modes as fallback) |
| waveguide crossover | f_c | rule: 1.05 × top mode |
| waveguide loss | σ₁ₖ, β | recording's band T60s above the bank |
| dispersion | M₁, a₁, M₂, a₂, j | phase-domain grid |
| waveguide input | level, tilt | bands above the crossover, 0–2 s |

In total, (b) has 50 fitted numbers a point, 48 of them the noise. (c)
adds 12, 11 of them fitted (the crossover is a rule).

What these say:

1. **The seam in (a) is real, and it is a cliff, not a level step.** Broadband
   it is small (−2.0 to +0.1 dB, median by set). Above the bank's top mode it
   is −3.8 to −11.2 dB at the burst's end (worst −12.5, guitar G3). (b) and
   (c) have no handover, so nothing steps in the render. The same measure at
   the same instants reads −0.4 to +1.1 dB (medians by set): that is the
   model's own drift against the recording over those 20–60 ms. The worst is
   +10.9 on wurli C4, where the recording's top falls away in that window and
   the modes' does not.
2. **The early window is (a)'s.** (b) is 4.6–9.9 dB rms off the
   recording's band energy over 0–50 ms, against (a)'s 0.5–1.3. Most of it
   is **pitched energy the modes don't have in the first 150 ms**:
   - the prompt sound (partials louder early than their fitted exponential;
     the fit weighted the burst window at 0.3, because the burst was going
     to carry it);
   - the soundboard knock at 60–150 Hz (5–10 dB per band);
   - on bass notes, the partials above the bank (piano-vcsl A#1: 14.5 dB in
     (b), 11.6 in (c)).

   The floor bound keeps the noise from faking these. That is why (b)'s
   attack static is −7 to +1 dB (medians by set) rather than +8 to +38.
3. **The waveguide supplies the band above the bank, and doesn't double.**
   - **Above the top at 0.3–1 s,** piano-vcsl goes from −5.2 dB in (a) and
     −6.7 in (b) to −0.6 in (c); Iowa from −6.9 to −5.1; the Wurlitzer from
     −28.2 to −13.8.
   - **Missing partials at 0.3–1 s** on vcsl A#1 fall from 43 to 26.
   - **Doubling.** The waveguide's level at every bank mode's frequency is at
     most −58 dB against that mode (a beat of 0.01 dB). The one exception is
     piano-vcsl C3: −18 dB at one mode, worth up to a 1.0 dB beat, from the
     in-loop high-pass sitting at 0.8 × the crossover.
   - **The bass series is where it is weakest.** piano-vcsl A#1's partials
     are 0.80 of a spacing off on average; first-order sections can't bend
     it (their delay change sits at DC). Iowa A1 is at 0.21, and C3 and above
     at 0.07–0.30.
4. **Velocity, where (b) does something (a) can't.** Level and spectral
   centroid over each strike's first 200 ms, at velocity 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0:

   | note | (a) | (b) |
   |---|---|---|
   | piano-iowa C4 | −15 → −3 dB, 394–398 Hz: the burst is low-passed but the modes don't change | −19 → −3 dB, 313 → 402 Hz: darker when soft |
   | wurli C3 | 539 → 626 Hz | 396 → 599 Hz |

   On a layered world like piano-vcsl, the world's swings span only 0.91–1.0,
   so the felt has nothing to act on. (a) carries that world's velocity in
   its takes, and (b) would need velocity mapped to hammer speed over a wider
   range.

### A bug in today's runtime this found (not fixed: `core/` is not mine)

`ResonatorWorld::At()` takes a point's burst length and fade from the
point's **first** burst (kyk_resonate.h:1463–1465). A layered point plays the
burst(s) that bracket the swing, and their takes' bursts differ in length.
When the played burst ends before the modes' lead (taken from the first
burst) is over, the note has a hole.

**piano-vcsl C5 at velocity 1.0 is true silence at 70–80 ms:** the hard
take's burst is 70 ms long, but the modes come in at 80 ms. (a) scores
−27.6 dB at the seam and 192 dB on the envelope for that note. When the
played burst is the longer one, the modes and the recording double instead,
as on C3: the modes come in at 40 ms while the burst plays to 240 ms.

This is a scan of every card world: points with a dip over 20 dB, at
velocity 1.0 / 0.8 / 0.5.

| world | points with a dip | worst dip (dB) |
|---|---|---|
| piano-vcsl | 5 / 1 / 2 | −189 / −96 / −95 |
| marimba-iowa | 8 / 1 / 1 | −99 / −95 / −20 |
| ep | 4 / 0 / 0 | −98 |
| vibraphone-iowa | 2 / 2 / 2 | −99 / −98 / −98 |
| piano-salamander | 2 / 1 / 1 | −93 / −92 / −90 |
| guitar | 1 / 0 / 0 | −94 |

In the 4-voice passage the other voices mask it (no dip over 12 dB). It is a
dropout on a single note or in sparse playing.

**The fix:** the lead and fade of the burst actually played, taken in
`ResonatorVoice::Strike`, or the longest of the bracketing pair.

### Cost on the M7

Instruction counts come from `make -C proto armcost`: arm-none-eabi-g++ -O3,
cortex-m7, `-ffp-contract=off` as Kyklophoria's own armcost uses. The counts
are loop bodies.

| part | instructions | when |
|---|---|---|
| today's bank (pair loop) | 8 / mode / sample | always |
| today's burst player | 46 / playing slot / sample (1–4 slots) | 60–390 ms a strike |
| (b) noise bands, as written | 344 / sample (8.3k a block) | while the attack sounds (T60 ≤ 0.7 s) |
| (b) noise, band-outer as the bank is | 23 / band / sample (6.6k a block) | the same |
| (b) the strike's pulse and per-mode felt gains | ~21 / pulse sample + 12 / mode, once | a strike |
| (c) waveguide | 125 + 9 / dispersion section / sample (3.0k–6.5k a block at 0–16 sections) | while it rings (seconds, on the bass) |

A block is 24 samples. Per voice, the new parts cost:

- **(b):** about 6.6–8.3k per block while its noise sounds, where today's
  burst costs about 1.1–2.2k.
- **(c):** 3–6.5k per block more, for the note's life.

On 4 voices, with every voice in its attack and every waveguide at 16
sections, that is **53–59k of the 240k budget** on top of the banks, against
about 9k for today's bursts. Cheaper noise (8 bands, one decay) would halve
the noise's share.

Two strike-time costs don't count as they stand:

- **The waveguide's `Set()`.** On the desktop it uses std::complex, atan2
  and a 256-point loop-gain scan: roughly 20k cycles per strike on the M7 by
  estimate. On the module it would be precomputed per point at export, with
  only the delay rescaled for transposition.
- **The Hertz ODE** (RK4, pow) is desktop only; on the module it is the
  stored pulse.

### Size

Bursts are 96–100% of every world. A synthesised point is about 84 bytes
stored as bytes: the contact, ramp and arrival about 12; the noise 48 (a
level, two decays and a share per band); the waveguide about 24. As floats it
is 276 bytes.

## Listening list

All files are in `bench/threeway/`. Each is at the engine's 0.23 gain; the
`_rec` file is at the level the burst plays it, lined up on the strike.

1. **`piano-vcsl_C5_{a-recorded,b-synth,c-synth+waveguide,rec}`.** (a) has
   the hole at ~70–80 ms. Listen for a dropout, or a "double attack".
2. **`piano-vcsl_A#1_*` and `piano-iowa_A1_*`.** The bass cliff.
   - (a) plays the recording for 390 ms (393 ms on Iowa), then the band
     above 1.7 kHz falls away.
   - (b) has no cliff, and no zing either.
   - (c) keeps a zing above 1.7 kHz for seconds. **Listen for whether it is
     the piano's zing or a buzz:** on vcsl A#1 its partials are 0.8 of a
     spacing off; on Iowa A1, 0.2.
3. **`piano-vcsl_C3_*` and `piano-iowa_C3_*`.** The waveguide at its best
   tuned (0.19–0.30 of a spacing). vcsl C3 is the one note where it could
   beat against a mode (≤ 1 dB).
4. **`piano-iowa_C4_*` and `piano-vcsl_C4_*`.** The attack itself. (b)'s is
   synthesised; (a)'s is the hammer. Listen for a missing knock, or
   "plastic".
5. **`wurli_C2_*` and `wurli_C3_*`.** (c) keeps the harmonics above the bank.
   **`wurli_C5_b-synth`** builds over 18 ms; listen for a slow or soft
   attack.
6. **`guitar2_G3_*` and `guitar2_E4_*`.** (b) against (a) on a pluck. The
   waveguide never turns on for these recordings, so (c) = (b).
7. **`*_passage_{a-recorded,b-synth,c-synth+waveguide}`.** Pops at note
   changes, which is where you hear them. The vcsl (c) strikes on notes
   48–52 are the marginal ones.
8. **`piano-iowa_C4_velocity_{a-recorded,b-synth}` and `wurli_C3_velocity_*`.**
   Four strikes, soft to hard. Does (b)'s soft strike sound like a soft
   strike, or like a muffled one?

## What I would do next to make (b) or (c) beat (a)

1. **Refit the modes with the attack in the loss.** Set `MB_BURST_FLOOR` to
   1.0, or give each mode a two-stage decay. The fit was told the burst
   would carry the first 60 ms. Without a burst, the modes must carry the
   prompt sound, and that is most of (b)'s early-window gap. It is a GPU
   refit, so not tonight.
2. **The knock as 2–4 damped resonators** at 60–150 Hz, fitted on 0–100 ms,
   instead of the bottom noise bands. That is the other large early error.
3. **Second-order allpass dispersion** for the bass (Rauhala & Välimäki;
   Abel, Välimäki & Smith 2010), fitted over the recording's band (1.7–6 kHz
   on A#1). This would take the bass series from 0.8 of a spacing to under
   0.2. A pair of sub-band loops is the other way.
4. **The waveguide's input as a 2–3 band EQ,** not one tilt. Its level
   compromises between bands: A#1 sits about 5 dB under across 0.05–2 s.
5. **Contact length from the takes.** VCSL and Salamander have 3–4 layers,
   and the brightness difference between them identifies T and p. One take
   cannot.
6. **Cost.** Use band-outer noise with 8 bands and one decay, and precompute
   the waveguide per point at export. Then (c) on 4 voices is about 25–35k a
   block.
7. **Separately, and today, for (a): fix the burst-lead hole above.**
